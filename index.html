UNDER THE CANOPY

A model for asking better questions.

FirelineLab is an educational sandbox, not a calibrated wildfire forecast or a burn-planning tool.

01 / Two clocks, one landscape

Each step advances forest succession by one day and then runs four abstract fire-spread pulses. Fire pulses are not measured hours. Each cell is a 50 × 50 m stand (0.25 ha); the whole grid is 1,120 ha including water. One cell can contain brush, dead fuel, and a representative tree age.

Fast-forward compresses these steps; it does not change the equations. Fires can continue across ecological days. The timeline begins at elapsed day zero.

02 / Three ways fire travels

Neighbor threshold: a deliberately simple baseline. Fire spreads to adjacent stands whose tree age exceeds the maturity threshold, with a user-set probability. Wind and moisture do not affect neighbor propagation.

Surface + wind: spread probability combines brush and dead fuel, moisture, direction-dependent wind, terrain differences, and diagonal distance.

Ember + crown: adds wind-carried ignitions across gaps and increases spread between mature stands when the source fire is intense. Firebreaks stop direct surface spread, but embers can jump them.

03 / Forest growth & loss

Age increases with the growth multiplier. Brush grows toward a maximum; litter accumulates beneath trees and slowly decomposes. Recruitment is the annual probability of an empty stand establishing trees, with a 90-day post-burn delay. Fire consumes surface fuel, with intensity-dependent stand mortality and greater modeled resistance for mature stands.

The surface fuel index is 100 × (mean brush + 0.8 × mean dead fuel) / 1.8, with both fuel components in the 0–1 range. High-fuel stands have brush + 0.8 × dead fuel + 0.15 for tree presence above 0.95. Fuel, intensity, and accumulation scores are relative indices, not laboratory measurements. Stand counts are not individual tree counts. Species differences, smoke, suppression crews, buildings, and soil processes are not modeled.

04 / Treatment is an experiment

Scheduled prescribed fires ignite a line inside a rotating vertical treatment strip. Treatment share controls strip width, not a guaranteed area burned. Thinning removes the chosen fraction of tree stands in that strip and exports their biomass before ignition. Prescribed ignition starts with lower intensity, then follows the same spread equations and can escape the strip.

The first treatment occurs on the selected day of year 1 and repeats at the chosen interval. There is no weather-based safety gate. Painted firebreaks persist; vegetation does not regrow on them. The stream is nonflammable; embers can cross it.

05 / Repeatability & comparison

The seed determines terrain, vegetation, lightning locations, and daily weather variation. Separate random channels keep weather and lightning comparable across management strategies. Restore pre-burn reuses the original landscape while retaining current settings. Apply landscape creates a new baseline from the setup controls.

To include painted thinning or firebreaks in your baseline, make those edits before advancing time or igniting fire. Edits after a run starts are interventions and are removed on pre-burn reset. Saved experiment summaries do not restore running simulation states.

06 / Parallel forests & multiple seeds

The parallel lab freezes your current pre-burn landscape and runtime settings. Both arms advance together, with scheduled prescribed burning enabled only in the managed arm. Loading setup again captures later changes; resetting the pair reuses its frozen inputs.

Multi-seed tests keep the exact same forest, including painted baseline edits, and vary the stochastic seed across 20, 50, or 100 pairs. Weather and lightning match within each pair. The median change is the median of paired differences. The 10th–90th percentile band describes the sampled outcomes, not a confidence interval or real-world probability.

Original mature survival follows the starting mature cohort, including losses to fire and thinning. Replacement trees do not restore that survival percentage. Fire-caused stand losses exclude thinning. Higher cover and survival, or lower burned area, losses, and fuel, count as improvement only for that individual measure.

The latest completed multi-seed result persists locally. Reports embed maps, charts, settings, and results in a standalone HTML file with a browser print/PDF option. Multi-seed report maps show the first seed only, not an average forest.

07 / Inspired by fire science

The model reflects qualitative relationships between fuel, weather, terrain, surface fire, and spotting. Its coefficients are authored for exploration; they are not fitted to real-world observations or an implementation of Rothermel, FARSITE, or FVS.
